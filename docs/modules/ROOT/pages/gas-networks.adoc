= Gas networks
:description: Compressors, filling a gas line, boosters, and bringing a big line up in sections.

Read the xref:primer.adoc#_gas_is_different[primer's gas section] first.
The short version: gas compresses, so a gas line stores gas as pressure and takes time to fill.

NOTE: Gas properties are still being tuned.
The way gas networks behave won't change, but the times on this page are estimates and may change.

== Gas lines need compressors

Gas machines work at MPa.
A Blender's nitrogen inlet draws nothing below about 5.5 MPa gauge and wants 6.9 MPa.
That is far above what any gas source supplies; a Resource Well's nitrogen comes out at about 1.45 MPa.

A pipeline pump placed on a gas line works as a *compressor*.
It multiplies the absolute pressure at its suction by up to its ratio limit.
See xref:compressors.adoc[] for the numbers.

TIP: Because a compressor multiplies absolute pressure, it can do much more with a high suction pressure than a low one.

== Filling takes time

Pressure appears only once the line holds enough gas, and it fills no faster than the producers supply, less what is drawn off.

A compressor at the far end of a line that isn't full pulls its suction below atmospheric and runs near its ratio limit, moving little, until the line fills.

=== How long a line takes to pack

A gas line is never full the way a liquid line is.
It _packs_: gas goes in until the line reaches its working pressure.
A new or flushed line starts at atmospheric pressure, so the whole line has to pack up to a machine's inlet pressure before that machine runs.

Each 100 m of pipe holds a roughly fixed amount of gas for every MPa of pressure:

[cols="2,1,1"]
|===
| Gas | Mk1, per 100 m | Mk2, per 100 m

| Nitrogen Gas
| 1,200 std m³ per MPa
| 1,700 std m³ per MPa

| Rocket Fuel, Ionized Fuel
| 1,200 to 1,500 std m³ per MPa
| 1,800 to 2,100 std m³ per MPa

| Dark Matter Residue
| 70 std m³ per MPa
| 100 std m³ per MPa

| Excited Photonic Matter
| 35 std m³ per MPa
| 50 std m³ per MPa
|===

Rocket Fuel and Ionized Fuel take more per MPa as the pressure rises: the lower figure is near their producers' 2.1 MPa, the higher one near a Packager's 16.5 to 18.6 MPa.

So:

[.text-center]
*minutes ≈ (length ÷ 100 m) × pressure in MPa × std m³ per MPa ÷ flow in std m³/min*

.300 m of Mk2 to a Blender's pressure
====
300 m of Mk2 packed with Nitrogen Gas to a Blender's 6.9 MPa, at 300 std m³/min: 3 × 6.9 × 1,700 ÷ 300 ≈ 120 minutes.
====

A handy figure: Nitrogen Gas in Mk1 pipe at 120 std m³/min, one Blender's draw, takes about 10 minutes per MPa for each 100 m.

.Minutes to pack 100 m of empty pipe to the consumers' desired pressure, at 120 std m³/min
[cols="2,2,1,1"]
|===
| Gas | Consumer, desired pressure | Mk1 | Mk2

| Nitrogen Gas
| Blender, 6.9 MPa
| 69
| 99

| Rocket Fuel, Ionized Fuel
| Fuel-Powered Generator or Refinery, 6.9 MPa
| 78
| 110

| Dark Matter Residue
| Particle Accelerator, 68.9 MPa
| 40
| 57

| Excited Photonic Matter
| Quantum Encoder, 68.9 MPa
| 20
| 29
|===

Twice the flow takes half the time; ten times the length takes ten times as long.

Reading these numbers:

* *The flow is the surplus.*
Only gas that isn't being used packs the line.
A line feeding machines while it packs fills at the supply minus their draw, which can be a small part of the supply.
* *They are the shortest times.*
The last part of the fill slows as the line nears the pressure of whatever feeds it.
And a compressor delivers less as its discharge packs, so the line past it takes longer than the compressor's starting rate suggests.
* *A line packs only up to the pressure of whatever feeds it.*
Every gas consumer needs more than any producer pushes, so the consumers' pressures are reached only past a compressor, at the flow the compressor delivers.
The line before the compressor packs first, to the producers' pressure.
* *A line already partly packed needs only the difference.*
Take off the time for the pressure it already has.
* *Std m³ is gas measured at atmospheric pressure,* the units the machines show.

=== The quicker layout: the compressor at the consumer's end

A compressor at the consumer's end needs the long line packed only to its own suction pressure; only the short stub past it packs to the consumer's.
A compressor at the source has to pack the whole line to the consumer's pressure.

.Four wells down a 1,075 m nitrogen line
====
Four Resource Well Extractors (480 std m³/min together) fed about 240 m of Mk1 and 800 m of Mk2 to a Mk2 compressor, with a Blender about 30 m past it.
One Mk2 reaches the Blender's 5.5 MPa from about 0.6 MPa at its suction, so the long line had to pack only to 0.6 MPa:
(2.4 × 1,200 + 8 × 1,700) × 0.6 ÷ 480 ≈ 20 minutes for the pipe.
In the game, with a Fluid Buffer on the line as well, the first gas reached the Blender at 29 minutes.

With the compressor at the wells, the whole line would have had to pack to 5.5 MPa: more than 3 hours.
====

== One line for all the wells

All the wells at a nitrogen extraction point can share one output line.
A single Mk2 pipe carries far more than a pressurizer's wells make.

== Long lines: boosters

Gas loses pressure to friction as it flows, and the loss feeds on itself.
As the pressure falls the gas expands, moves faster, and loses pressure faster still.
So a line that carries a lot of gas a long way arrives at a low pressure.

In the game you'll rarely have this problem, as the modeled lines can flow very high rates without much loss. However, if you run into this situation, a compressor partway along, a *booster*, lifts the pressure again; the gas past it is denser and slower and loses less. Real gas pipelines work this way, with compressor stations along their length.

* *The wells set the start.*
Their outlet holds at most about 1.45 MPa, so a line straight from them can lose no more than that before its far end reaches atmospheric.
A compressor near the wells raises the whole line's pressure, so the same flow loses less.
* *Put the booster where the suction is still well above atmospheric,* not at the far end of a line that has run down.
* *Compressors in series multiply:* two Mk2s reach about 7.9 × 7.9 times the first one's absolute suction pressure, less the line's losses between them.

== Bring a big line up in sections

The time to reach a machine's pressure grows with the volume being filled.
So split the line with valves and let the part nearest the compressor come up first; its machines start running while the rest waits.

. Valve off anything that adds volume without needing gas yet: a Fluid Buffer, or a machine whose inlet needs far more pressure than the others.
. Close a valve just past the first few consumers.
. Open each next valve *only part way*.
A gas valve holds the flow limit you set, in std m³/min.
Set it to what the producers make, minus what the running machines take.
Opened fully, the next section's low pressure pulls the running section below its machines' minimum and they stop.
. Open the Fluid Buffer last.

.A Rocket Fuel line
====
Two Blenders (200 std m³/min together) fed a Mk2 compressor and, past it, about 850 m of pipe, mostly Mk1, to fifteen Fuel-Powered Generators and a 400 m³ Fluid Buffer.
Rocket Fuel in Mk1 pipe takes about 1,300 std m³ per MPa per 100 m at the generators' 5.5 MPa.

* All at once, the pipe alone takes at least 8.5 × 5.5 × 1,300 ÷ 200 ≈ 5 hours, and the Fluid Buffer about 2 hours more.
* In sections of about 200 m, with the buffer valved off, the first generators start after about 2 × 5.5 × 1,300 ÷ 200 ≈ 70 minutes.
====

== Very high pressures: the plasma chain

A Particle Accelerator on Dark Matter Residue draws nothing below 48.3 MPa gauge and reaches full rate at 68.9 MPa.
That takes *at least two stages* of compression: from a Converter's 2.8 MPa, one Mk2 reaches only about 18 MPa, and two in series about 107 MPa.

Compressing these plasmas costs far more power than nitrogen.
The two Mk2 stages an Accelerator needs draw about 16 to 17 MW together; the more gas they move, the more they draw.

== Gas in an existing save

Vanilla gas lines stop when FicstFluids is installed.
Vanilla has no pressure, so its gas machines ran on whatever its pipes held.
FicstFluids keeps that gas, but in a long line it is far too little, and those lines have no compressors.

Add compressors, then give the line time to fill.
